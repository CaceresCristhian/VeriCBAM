## PART B. Scientific and reporting fixes (in priority order)

### Step 6. Fix how Models D and B are reported (S1)

**Problem (verified):** Model D's probability is `1/(1+exp(z))`, which is exactly 0.5 at zero deviation, so about half of authentic years score above 0.5 by construction. That produces the reported 51.98% false-positive rate and 100% recall at every understatement size. It is a mapping artifact, not a property of historical evidence. Model B scores abstentions as exactly 0.50, and the `>= 0.5` rule counts them as flagged, which inflates its false-positive rate by about 10 points.

**Actions:**
1. **Stop using a fixed 0.5 cut-off to compare models.** Add `recall_at_fpr_5/10/20` (recall on positives at the score threshold that gives 5%, 10%, 20% false-positive rate on authentic negatives). I computed these approximately from your predictions file: historical-only ≈ 0.85 / 0.91 / 0.99, learned ≈ 0.84 / 0.92 / 0.96. Recompute them properly in `run_graded_evaluation.py` with bootstrap CIs.
2. **Make abstentions explicit.** Return `NaN` (not 0.50) when a stream abstains. Report FPR in two ways: (a) among non-abstaining cases, (b) abstention rate separately. Never count an abstention as a flag.
3. **Give Model D a meaningful probability** if you still want a probability column: fit a one-feature logistic (grouped CV) on the one-sided score, or keep it threshold-free and report only AUC/PR-AUC and recall at fixed FPR. Do not present it with ECE or FPR at 0.5 otherwise.
4. State the consequence plainly in the results note: *at matched false-positive rates the learned model does not improve on the historical-only model.*

**Tests:** abstentions never count as flags; recall@FPR monotone in FPR; D mapping at z = 0 is not exactly the cut-off.

### Step 7. Diagnose the steel floor false alarms (S2)

**Problem (verified):** 34 of 71 authentic steel plant-years (about 48%) breach the "conservative" floor. Five integrated plants (Eisenhüttenstadt, Bremen, Taranto, Krupp Mannesmann, Tata IJmuiden) breach it in all six years. The expert model gives 22% of real years a probability of 0.99 or higher through the LR = 1e6 override, which is why its AUC is 0.776 and its false-positive rate 22.6%.

**Diagnosis (do this before changing anything):**
1. Build a table per steel plant: nameplate capacity, reported CO₂, and the **utilization that would be needed to pass the floor** (`CO2 / (capacity × 1.35)`). For the five plants this is about 0.44–0.62; for Bruckhausen and Hochfeld it is about 0.06 and 0.16, which points to a capacity/boundary mismatch (nameplate capacity of a larger complex vs installation-level E-PRTR CO₂). This is a hypothesis; check each plant's registry note and the E-PRTR facility name.
2. Check whether E-PRTR CO₂ for these installations excludes transferred process gases (a boundary question). Record what you find in `docs/DATA_PROVENANCE.md`.
3. Run the same table for cement to confirm the problem is concentrated in steel.

**Fix options (choose after the diagnosis, and document the choice):**
- **(a) Applicability flag:** mark a plant `floor_not_applicable` when its authentic years breach the floor in most years, and let the stoichiometric stream abstain there. Report clearly that this uses the authentic years to judge applicability, and show results with and without it.
- **(b) Correct the inputs** where the registry capacity or route is wrong for the installation boundary, with a source for each change.
- **(c) Replace the hard override with a finite, capped LR** when the production proxy is uncertain, so one floor breach cannot dominate fusion on its own.
- **(d) Narrow the claim:** present the physics check as validated for cement only until steel is resolved.

Re-run the evaluation after the chosen fix and update the expert-fusion narrative (remove the "confidence gating dampens posterior scores" explanation, which the data does not support).
